Core Belief

“Computer science is not learned through passive consumption. It is mastered through deliberate creation, breaking code, and treating every runtime exception as a diagnostic discovery.”

Core Principles

Three Pillars
of Instruction

Pillar 01

The Debugging Mindset

Syntax errors and stack traces often induce anxiety in novice programmers. My pedagogy deconstructs debugging into an objective, systematic discipline. By teaching students how to read compiler errors, leverage stack traces, and isolate edge cases, failure shifts from a measure of aptitude to an expected step in the engineering cycle.

Pillar 02

Authentic Engineering Tooling

Classrooms often isolate students in proprietary sandboxes that differ drastically from professional environments. By integrating standard version control (Git), modern terminal workflows, and automated continuous integration (GitHub Classroom), students graduate with practical muscle memory applicable to collegiate computing and industry internships.

Pillar 03

Equitable Access & Broadening STEM

Computer science literacy must not remain an exclusive domain. Through culturally responsive computational units, collaborative pair-programming protocols, and proactive outreach, our department demystifies advanced tracks like AP CS A, actively dismantling barriers for underrepresented students and first-time coders.

Instructional
Methodology

01

Driver-Navigator Pair Programming

Students alternate roles daily—verbalizing logic, catching edge cases before compilation, and building collaborative communication essential to software teams.

02

Test-Driven Lab Exercises

Assignments are accompanied by student-written unit tests, requiring learners to define expected outputs and test boundary conditions before implementing algorithms.
